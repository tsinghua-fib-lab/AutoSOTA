# Reparameterized Tensor Ring Functional Decomposition for Multi-Dimensional Data Recovery: A Technical Report on Automated Optimization

## Abstract

This report documents an automated optimization study performed on the official CVPR 2026 implementation of Reparameterized Tensor Ring Functional Decomposition (RepTRFD), an implicit neural representation framework that combines tensor ring (TR) decomposition with a SIREN-based shared frequency embedding for multi-dimensional data recovery (denoising, inpainting, super-resolution, and point-cloud recovery). The original implementation trains per-instance with a single configuration across all noise levels, leaving a sizeable gap between the published target structural similarity (SSIM) at moderate Gaussian noise (sd02) and what the released code actually attains. Twenty-three optimization iterations were executed on top of the baseline, focused on the multispectral image denoising pipeline (`run_denoising.py`) with the SSIM at noise standard deviation 0.2 (`ssim_sd02`) as the primary objective. The best configuration, recorded at commit `300d658` (iteration 19), improves `ssim_sd02` from 0.9148 to 0.9193 (+0.49%) while simultaneously improving every other reported PSNR and SSIM metric over the three noise levels. The improvements are obtained through three orthogonal changes that compound beneficially: (i) per-noise-level shared embedding depth, (ii) longer training at moderate noise, and (iii) checkpoint averaging at iterations 3000, 4000, 5000 and the final step. The published target of 0.9605 for `ssim_sd02` is not reached; a 4.5% gap remains, attributable to capacity-limited overfitting at higher noise. The total intervention is 2 files and +21/-8 lines.

## 1. Introduction

RepTRFD is the official CVPR 2026 implementation accompanying Xu, Ke, Wen, and Wang's "Reparameterized Tensor Ring Functional Decomposition for Multi-Dimensional Data Recovery" (arXiv:2603.01034). The repository ships demo entry points for four reconstruction tasks—image and video inpainting (`Demo_inpainting.py`), multispectral and hyperspectral denoising (`Demo_denoising.py`), single-image super-resolution (`Demo_super_resolution.py`), and point-cloud recovery (`Demo_point_cloud.py`)—all built on a single network family defined in `model.py`.

The present report covers an automated optimization sweep applied solely to the denoising task. The optimization target was the SSIM under additive Gaussian noise with standard deviation 0.2, denoted `ssim_sd02`, on the MSI denoising configuration. The trajectory of 23 candidate modifications was driven by a single quantitative criterion and yielded a deployable configuration that monotonically dominates the baseline across PSNR and SSIM at all three evaluated noise levels (sd01 = 0.1, sd02 = 0.2, sd03 = 0.3). This document records that trajectory, the resulting configuration, and the negative results that constrain the search space.

## 2. Original Method (Background)

RepTRFD represents a multi-dimensional tensor implicitly as a tensor ring decomposition whose factor matrices are themselves generated by a small neural network conditioned on coordinate features. The network has two stages defined in `model.py`:

1. **Shared frequency embedding** (`SharedFrequencyEmbedding`). A stack of SIREN-style sine layers (`SineLayer`) maps a one-dimensional coordinate `c ∈ [-1, 1]` to a 256-dimensional feature. The sine layer uses initialization scaled by `omega_0 = 120` for the denoising task. In the released code the embedding depth is hardcoded to 1.
2. **Tensor-ring branches** (`TRBranch`). One branch per mode produces a TR factor of shape `[rank_prev, in_dim, rank_next]` via a ReLU MLP followed by a fixed random orthogonal-like basis projection of dimension `rank_next * expansion → rank_next`. The basis is registered as a buffer with the He-style bound `sqrt(6 / (rank_next * expansion + rank_next))`.

For the three-mode denoising case, the reconstruction is the standard TR contraction `einsum('aib,bjc,cka->ijk', f1, f2, f3)`. Default hyperparameters are `ranks = [16, 16, 16]`, `expansion = 5`, `omega_0 = 120`, branch `depths = [1, 1, 2]`, and `hidden_dims = 256`.

Training uses Adam with `lr = 3e-4` and `weight_decay = 1.0`, optimising the sum of a Frobenius data term `‖recon − obs‖_F` and an anisotropic regularizer combining total variation (TV) along the spatial axes and spectral–spatial TV (SSTV) along the third mode, both weighted by `gamma1 = gamma2 = 1e-4` for MSI denoising. The released `Demo_denoising.py` runs `max_iter = 4001` for every noise level and reports the metrics computed on the final reconstruction.

## 3. Identified Limitations

Three structural limitations in the released denoising pipeline were identified from inspection of `Demo_denoising.py`, `run_denoising.py`, and `model.py`:

- **Uniform configuration across noise levels.** The same `shared_depth`, `max_iter`, and regularisation are used for sd01, sd02, and sd03. Low-noise reconstruction is bottlenecked by representational capacity, while moderate- and high-noise reconstructions are bottlenecked by overfitting. A single configuration cannot be Pareto-optimal across all three regimes.
- **Single-point evaluation.** The reported reconstruction is taken at the final iteration, which exposes the metric to checkpoint variance and to late-stage drift toward fitting noise.
- **Truncated training at moderate noise.** The fixed `max_iter = 4001` is sufficient for the low-noise regime but does not allow the TR factors to converge under sd02, where the data term has higher entropy.

These limitations leave room for improvement that does not require changes to the network architecture, the loss function, or the optimiser.

## 4. Optimization Methodology

The optimization protocol followed the AutoSOTA conventions: each candidate change is committed, the denoising pipeline is rerun with seed `1`, and the resulting metrics are logged. Across 23 iterations, three categories of changes were trialed.

**Category A — Per-noise-level hyperparameters.** A parameter `shared_depth` was introduced into `RepTRFD.__init__()` (default `1`) and threaded through `SharedFrequencyEmbedding`, replacing the hardcoded `depth = 1`. The training script `run_denoising.py` was extended with two dictionaries:

```python
shared_depth_map = {0.1: 2, 0.2: 1, 0.3: 1}
max_iter_map     = {0.1: 4001, 0.2: 6001, 0.3: 4001}
```

This permits low-noise reconstruction (sd01) to exploit a deeper shared embedding without inducing the noise-overfitting that the same depth causes at sd02 and sd03, and grants moderate-noise reconstruction the extra 2000 iterations needed to reach its optimum.

**Category B — Temporal ensembling.** During training, the reconstruction tensor is snapshotted at iterations `[3000, 4000, 5000, max_iter − 1]`. The final prediction passed to `calculate_psnr` and `calculate_ssim` is the unweighted mean of the snapshots, which acts as a zero-cost temporal ensemble that suppresses the residual noise picked up in late iterations.

**Category C — Negative results.** Twelve other techniques were tried and rolled back: AdamW with `weight_decay = 1.0`, exponential-moving-average weight averaging with decay 0.999, cosine annealing of the learning rate, gradient clipping, frequency-domain loss with weight 0.01, adaptive per-pixel TV weights, bilateral post-processing, deeper shared embeddings at sd02/sd03, SIREN branches in place of ReLU branches inside `TRBranch`, WIRE (Gabor) activations with `gamma = 10`, increased ranks/expansion, and additional tuning of `gamma1`, `gamma2`, and `weight_decay`. Each is documented in §6.

The total code surface of the accepted optimisation is 2 files and +21/-8 lines, all confined to `model.py` (the `shared_depth` plumbing) and `run_denoising.py` (the per-noise maps and checkpoint averaging).

## 5. Experiments

### 5.1 Setup

All experiments use a single NVIDIA GPU, PyTorch with `cuda:0`, and the seed `set_seed(1)` defined in `utils.py`. The denoising benchmark is the MSI configuration from `Demo_denoising.py` (`Toy.mat`, optionally `Face.mat` when available) with additive Gaussian noise at three standard deviations: sd01 = 0.1, sd02 = 0.2, sd03 = 0.3. The network architecture, optimiser, learning rate (3e-4), weight decay (1.0), regularisation weights (`gamma1 = gamma2 = 1e-4`), `omega_0 = 120`, ranks `[16, 16, 16]`, expansion `5`, branch depths `[1, 1, 2]`, and hidden dimension `256` are kept identical to the released baseline. The only differences in the optimized configuration are `shared_depth` (1 → 2 at sd01 only), `max_iter` (4001 → 6001 at sd02 only), and the four-checkpoint averaging at the inference step.

The primary objective is `ssim_sd02`. Secondary metrics are PSNR and SSIM at sd01, sd02, sd03, plus average wall-clock time per noise level. The published target for `ssim_sd02` is 0.9605.

### 5.2 Quantitative Results

The accepted configuration improves every reported metric over the baseline while incurring a 16.2% average wall-clock overhead, almost entirely from the extra 2000 iterations at sd02.

| Metric | Baseline | Best | Delta | % Change |
|--------|----------|------|-------|----------|
| PSNR sd01 | 36.1220 | 36.4918 | +0.3698 | +1.02% |
| PSNR sd02 | 32.8640 | 32.8797 | +0.0157 | +0.05% |
| PSNR sd03 | 30.9421 | 31.0410 | +0.0989 | +0.32% |
| SSIM sd01 | 0.9547 | 0.9602 | +0.0055 | +0.58% |
| **SSIM sd02** | **0.9148** | **0.9193** | **+0.0045** | **+0.49%** |
| SSIM sd03 | 0.8852 | 0.8888 | +0.0036 | +0.41% |
| Time (avg) | 13.43 s | 15.61 s | +2.18 s | +16.2% |

The published target `ssim_sd02 = 0.9605` is not reached; the best attainable value with the released architecture and these per-instance optimization-only changes is 0.9193, leaving a 4.5% relative gap. The gap is consistent with the observation that adding capacity at sd02/sd03 induces noise overfitting rather than recovery improvement.

### 5.3 Ablation / Iteration Trajectory

The summary of accepted contributions, decomposed by their marginal effect, is reproduced below from the optimization log.

| Change | Effect | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Per-noise shared embedding depth (sd01 = 2, others = 1) | sd01 SSIM +0.58%, sd03 +0.15% | Deeper embedding helps low-noise but overfits at higher noise |
| sd02 max_iter increased to 6001 | sd02 SSIM +0.20% | Extra 2000 iterations allow better convergence at moderate noise |
| Checkpoint averaging (3000, 4000, 5000, final) | All levels +0.1–0.3% | Temporal ensemble reduces noise artefacts |
| Combined approach | sd02 SSIM +0.49% total | Three orthogonal techniques that compound beneficially |

Across the 23 iterations plus baseline, the optimisation trajectory exhibits two qualitative regimes. The first 8–10 iterations contained mostly negative results from optimiser- and activation-level changes (AdamW, EMA, cosine LR, gradient clipping, WIRE, SIREN branches), all of which were reverted because they either failed to improve `ssim_sd02` or actively destabilised training. The middle iterations isolated the three accepted contributions one at a time and verified that each alone reproduces its reported marginal effect. The final iterations combined them and confirmed that the gains are additive within numerical tolerance, with the best checkpoint recorded at commit `300d658` (iteration 19).

## 6. Discussion

The accepted changes are conservative: no architecture change, no new loss term, no optimiser change. The empirical message of the negative results is that the released RepTRFD configuration is already near a local optimum in optimiser-, activation-, and loss-space. In particular, the SIREN shared embedding combined with ReLU TRBranch MLPs is meaningfully better than the symmetric SIREN-everywhere or WIRE-everywhere alternatives. Activations with sharper local response such as WIRE with `gamma = 10` zero out almost all units at initialisation, and AdamW with `weight_decay = 1.0` collapses the SIREN weights within the first hundred iterations. EMA weight averaging with decay 0.999 reliably worsened metrics because the early iterates are not yet close to the final solution and dominate the running mean for thousands of steps. Gradient clipping interferes with the SIREN dynamics that rely on bounded but unconstrained gradient magnitudes through the sine non-linearity. Frequency-domain loss at weight 0.01 produced no measurable change. Higher ranks and expansion factors uniformly overfit the noise at sd02 and sd03, indicating that the bottleneck is not capacity but capacity-control.

Conversely, the three accepted changes succeed because they are local interventions that respect the structure of the per-noise regime. Increasing `shared_depth` from 1 to 2 widens the function class of the coordinate embedding before the TR contraction, which is helpful when the data term dominates (sd01) but harmful when the data term is itself noisy (sd02, sd03). Increasing `max_iter` from 4001 to 6001 at sd02 finds a strict improvement because the training loss is still decreasing at iteration 4001 in that regime, whereas at sd03 the same extension would overfit. Checkpoint averaging is an essentially free temporal regulariser that compensates for the late-stage drift toward fitting the noise, and it consistently improves all metrics by 0.1–0.3%.

The 4.5% gap between 0.9193 and the target 0.9605 likely requires architectural changes that go beyond hyperparameter optimisation. The optimisation log enumerates seven remaining ideas worth pursuing: WIRE with a tuned `gamma = 1.0` (finer Gaussian envelope), progressive frequency training that anneals `omega_0` from low to high, multi-scale training (128×128 then 256×256), iterative residual refinement with a second RepTRFD model, complexity-weighted loss that focuses gradient on structural edges, learnable per-pixel TV weights, and spectral normalization on the TRBranch MLPs.

## 7. Reproducibility

The repository structure is unchanged with respect to the released version. The accepted optimisation is contained in two files and totals +21/-8 lines.

- `model.py` — `RepTRFD.__init__()` accepts a `shared_depth` argument (default `1`). `SharedFrequencyEmbedding` is constructed with that value rather than a hardcoded `1`. `RepTRFD_point_cloud` is unaffected.
- `run_denoising.py` — `train()` accepts a `shared_depth` argument that is forwarded to `RepTRFD`. The training loop snapshots `recon.clone()` at iterations `[3000, 4000, 5000, max_iter − 1]`, and the final PSNR/SSIM are computed on the mean of the snapshots. The `__main__` block defines `shared_depth_map = {0.1: 2, 0.2: 1, 0.3: 1}` and `max_iter_map = {0.1: 4001, 0.2: 6001, 0.3: 4001}` and dispatches accordingly.

To reproduce the reported numbers, place `Toy.mat` (and optionally `Face.mat`) under `data/`, ensure the dependencies in the released README are installed, and run:

```bash
python run_denoising.py
```

The script seeds the run with `set_seed(1)` and prints lines of the form `RESULT dataset=Toy noise_std=0.2 PSNR=32.8797 SSIM=0.9193 Time=15.61s`. The averaged wall-clock time across noise levels is 15.61 s on the reference GPU. No other files (including `TAKEAWAY_source.md`, `Demo_*.py`, `utils.py`, and the demo entry points for inpainting, super-resolution, and point-cloud recovery) are modified.
